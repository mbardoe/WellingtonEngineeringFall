# Engineering CAD and Design

This repository contains course materials, worksheets, assignments, and project resources for the 2026–27 **Engineering CAD and Design** course.

## Course Purpose

Students will use CAD as an engineering tool to model ideas precisely, predict how parts interact, communicate designs, manufacture physical objects, and manage the process of testing and improving a design. Students will create parametric models that respond predictably to changing requirements and use versioning to document and evaluate design decisions.

The course uses **Onshape** for CAD modeling and assemblies and **PrusaSlicer** to prepare parts for FDM 3D printing.

## Course Learning Goals

By the end of the term, students will be able to:

- Create accurate, fully constrained sketches and use them to build three-dimensional parts.
- Build a CAD model from measurements or a dimensioned drawing.
- Create engineering drawings that communicate the information needed to reproduce a part.
- Prepare a CAD model for FDM 3D printing and account for manufacturing constraints.
- Select tolerances and clearances using evidence from physical tests.
- Build assemblies and use mates to test the motion and interaction of parts.
- Create robust parametric models that respond predictably to changes.
- Use named versions, branches, and change logs to document design development.
- Test a physical design, evaluate the results, and make an evidence-based revision.

## Course Units

| Unit | Meetings | Focus | Principal Product |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. CAD Foundations and Precise Parts | 1–10 | Sketching, constraints, features, measurement, design intent, and engineering drawings | Reverse-engineered part and engineering drawing |
| 2. Designing for 3D Printing | 11–18 | FDM constraints, slicing, tolerances, fabrication, testing, and iteration | Useful 3D-printed object with a tested revision |
| 3. Assemblies and Interacting Parts | 19–21 | Mate connectors, motion, interference, and assembly documentation | Working Onshape assembly |
| 4. Parametric Design and Version Management | 22–29 | Variables, configurations, change testing, named versions, branches, and design decisions | Parametric product family with documented design history |

## Day-by-Day Plan: Term 1

### Week 1 — Course Launch

- **Day 1 — August 20:** Course introduction, the engineering design process, and a short design challenge

### Week 2 — Onshape and Measurement

- **Day 2 — August 25:** Onshape documents, Part Studios, navigation, units, and basic sketches
- **Day 3 — August 27:** Measurement workshop and dimensioned technical sketches *(45-minute class)*
- **Day 4 — August 28:** Basic features: extrude, remove, fillet, chamfer, and hole

### Week 3 — Constraints and Design Intent

- **Day 5 — September 1:** Sketch constraints, design intent, and predictable model changes
- **Day 6 — September 3:** File organization, history, naming, comments, and CAD skill check *(45-minute class)*
- **Day 7 — September 4:** Build a part from a supplied dimensioned drawing

### Week 4 — Reverse Engineering

- **Day 8 — September 9:** Measure and begin modeling a physical part
- **Day 9 — September 11:** Complete and verify the reverse-engineered model

### Week 5 — Engineering Drawings and Fabrication

- **Day 10 — September 15:** Engineering drawings, standard views, dimensions, and title blocks
- **Day 11 — September 17:** CAD-to-printer workflow with PrusaSlicer

### Week 6 — Designing for FDM Printing

- **Day 12 — September 22:** Overhangs, bridges, wall thickness, supports, orientation, and strength
- **Day 13 — September 24:** Design a tolerance and clearance test coupon *(45-minute class)*
- **Day 14 — September 25:** Measure printed coupons and establish practical fit guidelines

### Week 7 — Useful-Object Project

- **Day 15 — September 29:** Identify a need, examine examples, gather measurements, and define criteria
- **Day 16 — October 1:** Create the first CAD model and review design intent, printability, and tolerances

### Week 8 — Fabrication, Testing, and Revision

- **Day 17 — October 6:** Peer design review, revision, slicing, and fabrication preparation
- **Day 18 — October 8:** Test the printed object and document an evidence-based revision

### Week 9 — Assemblies

- **Day 19 — October 14:** Onshape assemblies, mate connectors, and fastened, revolute, and slider mates
- **Day 20 — October 16:** Multi-part mechanism and assembly-motion challenge

### Week 10 — Assembly Documentation and Parametric Design

- **Day 21 — October 20:** Interference, range of motion, assembly views, and exploded views
- **Day 22 — October 22:** Launch the Parametric Product Family Project and audit an existing model *(45-minute class)*
- **Day 23 — October 23:** Variables, expressions, relationships, symmetry, patterns, and feature order

### Week 11 — Configurations, Versions, and Branches

- **Day 24 — October 27:** Create and stress-test multiple configurations or size variations
- **Day 25 — October 29:** History, workspaces, named versions, and branches *(45-minute class)*
- **Day 26 — October 30:** Develop a meaningful alternative design on a branch

### Week 12 — Final Design Review

- **Day 27 — November 4:** Compare the baseline and alternative designs and make a documented design decision
- **Day 28 — November 5:** Final testing, engineering drawing, parameter table, and change log *(45-minute class)*
- **Day 29 — November 6:** Final presentations and design review

## Homework

Students will generally complete two approximately 30-minute practice blocks each week. During project cycles, a homework block may be used for project work. Some assignments may involve watching a short instructional video and responding with timestamped observations, connections, and questions.

Homework will not be due at the start of a 45-minute class meeting. For the specific assignment due at each class, see the course planner.

## Major Course Products

1. A reverse-engineered part and engineering drawing
2. A useful 3D-printed object with documented testing and revision
3. A working multi-part assembly
4. A parametric product family with named versions, an alternate-design branch, and a change log
