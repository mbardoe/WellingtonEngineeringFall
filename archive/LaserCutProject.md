# Group Laser Cutting Project: Design, Prototype, and Fabricate

**Group Size:** 2–3 students  
**Goal:** Work collaboratively to design, test, and fabricate a laser-cut object that can be assembled from flat parts.

You will create two versions of your design:
1. A **cardboard prototype** to test fit, assembly, and proportions.  
2. A **final version** cut from **plywood** after making design improvements.

---

## Project Options

Each group will choose **one** of the following design challenges:

### 1. Flat-Pack Furniture Challenge
Design a small piece of furniture that can be assembled without glue or screws.  
Examples: stool, phone stand, lamp frame, or small shelf.  
- Must fit together using **tabs, slots, or interlocking joints**.  
- Must stand on its own and support some weight (for its size).

### 2. Desk Organizer Challenge
Design a modular system that helps organize a student’s desk.  
Examples: pen holder, cable manager, sticky note tray, or multi-compartment organizer.  
- Should include **at least three pieces** that fit together or interlock.  
- Consider ergonomics and everyday usability.

### 3. Architectural Model Challenge
Design a small-scale building, bridge, or pavilion that can be cut and assembled from flat panels.  
Examples: abstract shelter, footbridge, or tower.  
- Focus on **form, structure, and assembly method**.  
- Use tabs and slots or layering to create stability.

---

## Project Phases

### Phase 1: Brainstorm & Sketch
- Discuss ideas with your group.  
- Make quick paper sketches of possible designs.  
- Identify how many parts you’ll need and how they’ll connect.  

### Phase 2: CAD Design in OnShape
- Model your design in **OnShape** using **2D sketches** for each part.  
- Use **assemblies** to test how parts fit together.  
- Prepare for laser cutting by keeping parts flat on the same plane.  

### Phase 3: Cardboard Prototype
- Export your OnShape sketches as **DXF files**.  
- Laser cut the first version from **cardboard**.  
- Assemble and test:
  - Do parts fit properly?
  - Are joints too tight or too loose?
  - Is it stable and functional?
- Record your observations and take photos.

### Phase 4: Redesign and Final Fabrication
- Return to OnShape and modify your design based on test results.  
- Adjust tab sizes or dimensions for plywood’s thickness.  
- Export final **DXF files**.  
- Laser cut your final model in **1/8" plywood**.  

### Phase 5: Presentation
Each group will present their project, including:
- OnShape model and drawings.  
- Photos of cardboard and plywood versions.  
- A short reflection (1–2 slides or paragraphs):
  - What changed between versions?
  - What did you learn about material properties and fit?

---

## Deliverables

- **OnShape document** shared with the teacher (with both versions visible).  
- **Cardboard prototype** (tested and photographed).  
- **Final plywood version.**  
- **Short group presentation** showing process, results, and reflection.

## Grading Rubric (50 points total)

| Category | Excellent (10)                                            | Good (8–9)                                          | Satisfactory (6–7)              |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Collaboration** | Group works effectively; roles clear; equal participation | Group works mostly well; minor gaps in contribution | Uneven participation            | 
| **Design Quality** | Creative, functional, and well-structured design          | Functional design with some creativity              | Basic design                    | 
| **CAD Work** | Accurate OnShape model; good use of sketches/assemblies   | Mostly accurate model                               | Some alignment or sizing issues | 
| **Iteration** | Clear improvements from cardboard to plywood              | Some revisions made                                 | Minimal revisions               | 
| **Fabrication** | Cleanly cut, well-assembled final model                   | Minor fit or quality issues                         | Parts fit roughly               | 
| **Presentation** | Clear, engaging, thoughtful reflection                    | Clear but missing details                           | Minimal explanation             |
