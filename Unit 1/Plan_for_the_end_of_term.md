Thanks — this gives a great big-picture framework. Since you’ve already built the scissors, dice, cake, and hinge box projects, the rest of the term can now move toward **applied workflows** (diagrams, versioning, fabrication) and then **culminate in a challenge + presentations**.

Here’s a suggested **week-by-week plan** based on your schedule:

---

## **Weeks 1–2 (3 meetings per week: 2×70 min, 1×45 min)**

**Focus:** Documentation & Collaboration in OnShape

* **Day A (70 min)**:

  * Lesson: **Creating diagrams/drawings in OnShape**
  * Students make a technical drawing of one of their previous projects (dice, scissors, hinged box).
  * Include dimensions, title blocks, and annotations.

* **Day B (70 min)**:

  * Lesson: **Version control in OnShape**
  * Show how to create versions, use branching, and restore older models.
  * Activity: Students intentionally create two different versions of a design (e.g., two lid styles for the hinged box).

* **Day C (45 min)**:

  * Wrap-up + quick challenge:

    * *“Redesign an earlier part in a new version, then create a technical drawing for it.”*
  * Share-out at the end.

---

## **Weeks 3–4 (2 meetings per week: 70 min each)**

**Focus:** Fabrication Workflows (3D Printing & Laser Cutting)

* **Day A (70 min)**:

  * Lesson: **From OnShape to PrusaSlicer to 3D printer**.
  * Demo: Export → Slice → Print (Ender-3 S1 Pro).
  * Activity: Students export one of their parts (dice or hinged box lid), slice it, and prepare a gcode file. (Optional: run some prints if time/resources allow.)

* **Day B (70 min)**:

  * Lesson: **From OnShape to Laser Cutter**.
  * Demo: Export DXF → import into LightBurn/RDWorks → prep for cut.
  * Activity: Students design a simple flat part (e.g., a nameplate, gear, or phone stand side panel) and export it for laser cutting.

---

## **Weeks 5–6 (3 meetings per week: 2×70 min, 1×45 min)**

**Focus:** Capstone Engineering Challenge & Presentations

* **Day A (70 min)**:

  * Introduce **Engineering Challenge** (open-ended).
  * Example: *“Design a device/structure that can hold and launch a ping pong ball,”* or *“Design a storage box with a unique locking mechanism.”*
  * Students brainstorm and begin CAD modeling.

* **Day B (70 min)**:

  * Project Work Session.
  * Teacher circulates, checking that students are using drawings, version control, and fabrication export skills.

* **Day C (45 min)**:

  * **Presentations**: Students give their **Engineering Careers Presentation (Final Draft)**.
  * Peer Q\&A to tie careers into their own design work.

* **Final Week**:

  * Engineering Challenge **Showcase Presentations** (each student presents their final design, workflow, and what they learned).
  * If possible, display some printed or cut prototypes.

---

## End-of-Term Deliverables

1. **OnShape Drawing** of one project.
2. **Versioned Model** showing design exploration.
3. **Prepared Files** for both 3D printing and laser cutting.
4. **Engineering Careers Final Presentation**.
5. **Capstone Challenge Project + Presentation**.
