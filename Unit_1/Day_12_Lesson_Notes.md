# Week 1, Day 1 Class Notes (70 min)

## Objectives
- Learn how to create **drawings/diagrams** from OnShape parts.  
- Practice dimensioning and labeling a CAD model for engineering communication.  
- Understand why technical drawings are important in engineering.

---

## Agenda

### 1. Warm-Up (10 min)
- Discussion prompt: *“Why do engineers make diagrams and drawings instead of just sharing the 3D model?”*  
- Collect student responses on the board (manufacturing, communication, documentation, standards).  
- Show a simple example of a technical drawing (with title block and dimensions).

---

### 2. Teacher Demo: Creating a Drawing in OnShape (15 min)
- Open one of your own sample parts (e.g., the Dice or Scissors Handle).  
- Walk through step by step:
  1. Right-click the Part → **Create Drawing**.  
  2. Choose **ANSI A size** sheet (landscape).  
  3. Insert base view, then project top, side, and isometric views.  
  4. Add dimensions using the **Dimension tool**.  
  5. Add a title block (auto-fills student name/document info).  
  6. Export as PDF.  
- Highlight: **views, dimensions, annotations**.

#### Notes for Area of Emphasis

* Orthographic Projections: Left-Right, Top-Bottom, Front-Rear
* Not perspective
* Think of a glass box.
* Use as few views as possible.
* 

---

### 3. Guided Practice: Student Drawing (20 min)
- Students open one of their own previous projects (Dice, Scissors Blade, Hinged Box, etc.).  
- Step-by-step together:
  1. Create a drawing from their part.  
  2. Add at least **three views** (front, top, isometric).  
  3. Add **key dimensions** (edge length, hole diameter, etc.).  
  4. Fill out the title block.

---

### 4. Independent Work (15 min)
- Students continue polishing their drawings.  
- Requirements:
  - At least 3 views.  
  - At least 5 dimensions.  
  - Title block completed.

---

### 5. Reflection + Share-Out (10 min)
- Quick write: *“What was hardest about making your first drawing? What do you think is valuable about having a drawing?”*  
- Volunteers share with class.  
- Teacher connects back to **real-world importance of diagrams**.

---

## Homework
- Explore the **Drawing palette** in OnShape.  
- Choose **one tool** we did *not* cover in class (examples: section view, broken view, detail view, auxiliary view, surface finish, geometric tolerances, hole callout).  
- Learn how it works (use OnShape help, YouTube tutorials, or trial-and-error).  
- Prepare a **2–3 minute mini-tutorial** for the next class where you show:
  1. What the tool does.  
  2. How to use it on a drawing.  
  3. Why an engineer might need it.

---

## Materials
- Projector with sample drawing.  
- Student laptops with OnShape.  
- Example technical drawing (printed or PDF).
