# Final Project: Design, Iterate, and Build

For your final project, you will design, model, and present an object of your choice.  
The project should be **familiar**, something people use or interact with regularly, and it should involve **both 3D printed and laser-cut parts**.  

You will go through an **iteration cycle**: design → test → improve → present.

---

## Requirements

1. **Choose a Project**
   - Pick something familiar and fun to design. Examples include:
     - A custom board game (box + playing pieces)  
     - A puzzle (interlocking or 3D brain teaser)  
     - A phone or device stand  
     - A simple musical instrument or sound toy  
     - A box with a non-motorized locking mechanism  
     - A tabletop launcher (ping pong ball, marble, or coin)  
   - You may also propose your own idea, but it must include both **laser-cut** and **3D-printed** elements.

2. **Model in OnShape**
   - Create a full CAD model of your design.  
   - Use **drawings** and **version control** to document your work.  
   - Your OnShape document should include at least **two iterations** (Version 1 and Version 2).

3. **Fabrication Files**
   - Prepare at least one **STL file** (for 3D printing) or **DXF file** (for laser cutting).  
   - Create an engineering diagram for at least one part of your design.  
   - Check sizes and tolerances carefully before exporting.

4. **Prototype**
   - If possible, produce a physical prototype using the 3D printer and laser cutter.  
   - Test it and note what worked and what needs improvement.

5. **Presentation**
   - Present your final design to the class. Your presentation should include:
     - Your chosen project and why you picked it.  
     - A walkthrough of your CAD model.  
     - Evidence of iteration (what you changed between versions).  
     - Fabrication files (show your STL/DXF outputs).  
     - Your physical prototype (if available).  
     - Reflection: *What did you learn about design through iteration?*

---

## Deliverables

- **OnShape CAD Document** (with at least two iterations).  
- **STL and/or DXF files** prepared for fabrication.  
- **Engineering Diagram** for parts of your design.
- **Prototype** (if possible).  
- **Final presentation** (5–7 minutes).  

---

## Timeline

- **Week 5**: Project introduction, brainstorming, and first design work.  
- **Week 6**: Iteration and project work sessions.  
- **Final Week**: Presentations and showcase.  

---

## Grading Rubric (100 points)

| Category              | Excellent (20)                                    | Good (15–19)                     | Satisfactory (10–14)                | Needs Work (0–9)                   |
|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Creativity**        | Original idea, highly engaging design             | Solid idea with some creativity  | Familiar or common idea              | Minimal creativity or copied work  |
| **OnShape Modeling**  | Fully detailed, drawings + versions clear         | Mostly detailed, some gaps       | Basic model, limited details         | Incomplete model                   |
| **Iteration**         | Clear improvement between versions                | Some changes, moderate growth    | Minor tweaks only                    | No iteration shown                 |
| **Fabrication Files** | STL + DXF + Eng. Dia. correct, ready to cut/print | Minor errors in setup            | Only one type of file prepared       | No fabrication files                |
| **Prototype**         | Functional, well-tested physical build            | Printed/cut but needs refinement | Partial prototype                    | No prototype                       |
| **Presentation**      | Clear, engaging, well-structured                  | Clear but missing some depth     | Basic, limited explanation           | Very weak or missing presentation  |

## Challenge (Optional)
- Add decorative or personalized elements.  
- Combine multiple fabrication methods (laminated layers, hybrid joints).  
- Make your project modular (different configurations possible).
